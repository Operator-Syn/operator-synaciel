# Dalan visual language

## Core feeling

Make the interface feel like a designed instrument: editorial in its hierarchy, technical in its notation, warm in its accent, and quiet enough that the content remains legible. The visual system should create tension through scale and alignment, not through noise.

## Useful ingredients

- Near-black or deep charcoal field with warm cream text.
- One amber, ochre, or signal-orange accent used as a deliberate status or directional cue.
- Oversized display type paired with compact utility labels, metadata, or monospaced notation.
- Strong left edges, indexed sections, thin rules, measured gaps, and a visible column/grid logic.
- Technical diagrams, rails, nodes, coordinates, or schematic marks as a restrained identity layer.
- One-page or long-form narrative flow when the content benefits from progression.
- Subtle entrance, hover, and scroll transitions that reveal structure without performing for their own sake.

## Translate, do not copy

| Observed principle | Product translation | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Oversized type creates an editorial entry point | Let the title carry the first hierarchy and test its wrapping at narrow widths | A headline so large that the action or context disappears |
| Warm accent creates a signal | Reserve the accent for links, active states, indexes, or one graphic anchor | Painting every control and border orange |
| Thin rules make the grid visible | Use rules to separate real sections and align metadata | Turning every element into a boxed card |
| Technical marks create character | Tie diagrams or notation to the product's story or data | Random fake telemetry and decorative pseudo-UI |
| Long-form flow supports case studies | Sequence context, work, evidence, and contact | Forcing every product into a single endless scroll |
| Restrained motion gives the page a pulse | Animate state change, focus, or hierarchy with a small budget | Scroll hijacking, perpetual loops, or motion that shifts layout |

## Contrast hierarchy

Define three levels before styling:

1. **Signal:** the page title, primary action, active item, or current status.
2. **Structure:** section labels, navigation, project names, dividers, and supporting controls.
3. **Atmosphere:** diagrams, texture, secondary metadata, and low-contrast decoration.

Atmosphere must never compete with signal. If everything is loud, reduce color and ornament before reducing legibility.

## Adaptation test

The direction is working when the page is recognizable with the decorative layer removed. The type scale, alignment, spacing, and accent logic should still carry the identity.
